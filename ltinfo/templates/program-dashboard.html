At a glance

Tahoe yellow cress on the shores of Lake Tahoe

Tahoe yellow cress (Rorippa subumbellata) grows only on the sandy beaches of Lake Tahoe. Surveys each year record which of the known sites hold plants. Lake level drives the count: high water covers beach habitat and fewer sites are occupied.

How it is measured

Annual site surveys

Sites

A fixed list of beach sites where the plant has been recorded since surveys began in 1978. New sites are added when plants are found elsewhere.

Survey

Each site is walked during the growing season and the number of plants is counted. A site is occupied when at least one plant is found.

Lake level

Lake Tahoe surface elevation from the Tahoe City gage, reported with each survey year because it sets how much beach is exposed.

Plant counts

Plants counted at each site

Per-site counts give a finer picture than occupancy alone. This section fills in when the count field is confirmed and documented.

About the program. The Tahoe Yellow Cress monitoring program tracks the number of occupied sites each year under the Vegetation Preservation threshold category, in partnership with the Tahoe Yellow Cress Adaptive Management Working Group.
