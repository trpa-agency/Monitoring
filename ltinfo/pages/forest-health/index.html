Forest Health

Three programs, one forest

TRPA tracks the condition of the Basin's forests through three monitoring programs. Forest Structure describes what the conifer forest is made of and how dense it stands. Fire Dynamics follows the fire the forest would carry and the work done to change that. Subalpine Conifer watches the highest stands, which were never logged and are held to a standard of no degradation. Together they inform the Forest Health threshold standards and the Environmental Improvement Program's forest work.

The programs

What each program measures

Each dashboard opens in the program's Dashboard card on Lake Tahoe Info, or on its own from the link.

Forest Structure

Seral stage, canopy cover, and stand density of conifer forest by forest type, from the Sierra Nevada Regional Resource Kit remote sensing products, compared with the desired ranges in the Forest Health threshold standards.

  • Acres by seral stage and forest type
  • Canopy cover and stand density classes
  • Change between Resource Kit vintages

Fire Dynamics

Modeled fire severity by management zone, the wildland urban interface defense zone, fuel reduction treatments completed through the Environmental Improvement Program, and the Basin's wildfire history.

  • Acres by modeled fire severity and zone
  • Treated acres by year and treatment type
  • Wildfire perimeters and acres burned

Subalpine Conifer

Mapped extent of subalpine forest above roughly 8,500 feet, its dominant species and stand structure, who manages the land, and the ground plot network that will inform remote sensing of change over time.

  • Mapped acres and dominant species
  • Tree size class and ownership
  • Whitebark pine condition from plots, as surveys load

How they fit together

From remote sensing to the threshold standards

Forest Structure and Fire Dynamics are built on the same Sierra Nevada Regional Resource Kit rasters, so a change in one can be read against the other: a treated stand should show lower modeled severity in the next vintage, and a denser stand should show higher. Subalpine Conifer uses TRPA's vegetation mapping instead, because the Resource Kit was not built for the highest elevations, and it adds ground plots so the mapped extent can be checked against what is standing.

All three describe the record. The evaluation of that record against the standards happens on the Threshold Dashboard every four years, and the work that responds to it is tracked in the Environmental Improvement Program.

Resource Kit

The Sierra Nevada Regional Resource Kit is a set of statewide 30 meter rasters for vegetation structure, fire, and carbon, produced for the Wildfire and Forest Resilience Task Force and updated every few years.

Vegetation map

TRPA's vegetation type layer, built on U.S. Forest Service existing vegetation mapping with Basin attributes, classifies every mapped stand by California Wildlife Habitat Relationships type and size.

Ground plots

Fixed plots in the subalpine zone where crews record species, size, and condition, including white pine blister rust, to calibrate what the imagery shows.
